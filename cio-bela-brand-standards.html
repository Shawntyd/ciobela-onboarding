Section One
Professional Appearance
Not yet reviewed
▾
Our philosophy: As a 1099 independent contractor, Cio Bela does not dictate your wardrobe. However, your appearance is a direct reflection of the brand experience our clients expect. We ask that every contractor uphold a standard of professional presentation that is consistent with Cio Bela's Warm Minimalism aesthetic.
The Standard
Contractors are expected to present themselves in a manner that is clean, polished, and client-ready at all times while on Cio Bela premises. Your appearance communicates your brand — and while on our floor, it communicates ours as well.

Think refined, intentional, and professional. Cio Bela's aesthetic is Warm Minimalism — earthy tones, clean lines, understated elegance. Your personal style should complement — not contradict — this environment.
✓ Encouraged
Neutral tones, earth tones, or all-black attire
Clean, pressed, wrinkle-free clothing
Closed-toe shoes (sanitation & safety)
Hair styled and away from the face while working
Minimal, intentional accessories
Natural or polished nails that showcase your craft
✕ Not Appropriate
Visibly dirty, torn, or stained clothing
Graphic tees with offensive or controversial content
Open-toe shoes or flip-flops during service
Strong perfume or cologne (clients may have sensitivities)
Clothing that creates a distraction from the service environment
Unkempt, unwashed, or unstyled appearance
Personal Hygiene
Given the close-proximity nature of nail services, personal hygiene is non-negotiable. Contractors must arrive clean, odor-neutral, and client-ready. This includes clean hands and properly maintained nails. Arriving in active workout gear or immediately after the gym without freshening up is not appropriate for a client-facing environment.

Section Two
Station Setup & Cleanliness
Not yet reviewed
▾
Your station is your business. How you keep your space tells every client — and every passing eye — exactly who you are as a professional. Cio Bela's environment is intentionally curated. Your station should feel like it belongs here.
Station Presentation Standard
Your station must be clean, organized, and visually cohesive at all times — before, during, and after each client. Products should be neatly arranged. Surfaces should be free of dust, product buildup, and clutter. Your workspace is visible to every client who walks through the door, not just the client in your chair.
✓ Station Must-Haves
Clean, lint-free table surface at all times
Products stored neatly (not sprawled across the surface)
Implements in sealed storage between clients
A clean client cushion/armrest for each appointment
Trash emptied before it overflows
No personal food or beverages on the service surface
✕ Never Acceptable
Dirty implements left on the table between clients
Product bottles with dirty, crusted caps on display
Excessive personal items taking up service space
Leaving a messy station at end of shift
Using another contractor's supplies without permission
Storing personal belongings in common areas
Sanitation Protocol
Per TDLR regulations and your Contractor Agreement, all implements must be properly disinfected between every client using EPA-registered, TDLR-approved disinfectants. Single-use items (files, buffers, orangewood sticks) must be discarded after each client — never reused. Implements must be stored in a sealed container or UV sterilization case.

Your station disinfection is your sole responsibility. TDLR fines resulting from sanitation violations at your station will be charged back to you per your Contractor Agreement.
End-of-Shift Responsibility
Before leaving for the day, your station must be wiped down, disinfected, organized, and ready for your next appointment. This means: all product bottles closed and stored, surface cleaned, trash emptied, implements properly stored, floor around your station swept if needed. Leaving a messy station overnight is not permitted and may trigger the self-help cleaning charge outlined in your Contractor Agreement.

Section Three
Music & Ambiance
Not yet reviewed
▾
The Cio Bela ambiance is intentional. Our clients come here to decompress, feel beautiful, and escape. Every sensory detail — including sound — contributes to that experience. What plays in this space matters.
Salon Background Music
Cio Bela curates the salon's background music to maintain a consistent, upscale atmosphere. The general salon soundtrack is managed by Salon Management and reflects our brand aesthetic — think ambient, R&B, neo-soul, soft jazz, or curated lounge playlists.

Contractors may play their own music at their station via personal earbuds or low-volume personal speakers, provided it does not conflict with or overpower the salon's background music or create an audible distraction for neighboring stations or clients.
✓ Acceptable
Personal earbuds (one ear only while serving clients)
Low-volume ambient or background music at your station
Music that complements a calm, upscale environment
Checking with your client about their music preference
✕ Not Permitted
Playing explicit lyrics at audible volume in the salon
Music that overpowers the salon's background atmosphere
Aggressive, highly political, or controversial audio content
Video or podcast content played aloud at your station
Both earbuds in while serving a client (safety & engagement)
Volume & Noise
Conversation volume should be kept at a level that does not carry across the salon floor. Laughing, speaking loudly, or extended personal phone calls in the middle of the salon disrupts the environment. Phone calls should be taken briefly and, when possible, stepped away from the main floor. FaceTime or video calls with audio are not permitted in the service area.
Television & Video
Any television or shared screen content within the salon is managed by Salon Management. Contractors may not connect personal devices to shared screens or salon audio systems without explicit management approval.

Section Four
Client Communication Standards
Not yet reviewed
▾
Every client interaction is a brand touchpoint. How you greet, communicate with, and part from a client shapes their entire memory of Cio Bela. You are the experience.
Greeting & First Impressions
All clients entering the salon should be acknowledged within 30 seconds — even if you are mid-service. A simple "Welcome in, I'll be right with you!" goes a long way. Clients should never stand at the door feeling ignored. If you are not the contractor they are booked with, a warm acknowledgment and direction to the correct artist is still expected.
During Service
Your client's time in your chair is sacred. Give them your full attention and genuine presence. Limit personal phone use to essential communication only during service. Extended personal texting or scrolling social media while a client is in your chair is unprofessional and reflects poorly on Cio Bela as a whole.

Conversation should be warm, professional, and client-led. Follow their cues — some clients want to chat, some want to relax quietly. Both are valid. Avoid discussing controversial topics including politics, religion, or other contractors' personal business.
✓ Professional Communication
Warm, genuine greeting for every client
Clear consultation before every service
Honest expectations about timing and outcomes
Proactive communication if running behind
A warm send-off and rebooking prompt
✕ Unprofessional Communication
Ignoring or making clients wait without acknowledgment
Discussing other contractors' personal matters
Complaining about salon management to clients
Arguing with or dismissing a client's concerns
Soliciting clients to follow you to another location
Conflict Resolution
If a client is unhappy with their service, remain calm, listen fully, and do not become defensive. Offer what you can within reason. As an independent contractor, the resolution of service disputes is between you and your client directly — your clients pay you, not the salon. If you need guidance or support in handling a difficult situation, management is available to assist. Maintain professionalism at all times regardless of the outcome.
Client Privacy
Information shared by clients during their appointment — personal, medical, or otherwise — is confidential. Do not share, repeat, or discuss client information with other contractors, on social media, or in any public forum. Violations of client privacy may result in immediate termination of your contractor agreement.

Section Five
Social Media & Photography
Not yet reviewed
▾
Your content is your portfolio. Great content shot at Cio Bela benefits both of us — it builds your following and elevates our brand. We encourage you to share your work. We just ask that you do it with intention.
Photography of Your Work
You are encouraged to photograph and share your completed nail work on your personal social media. When posting work done at Cio Bela, we ask that you tag @CioBelaSalons (or the official handle provided by management) when content is captured on our premises or prominently features our space. This is not mandatory, but it is appreciated and mutually beneficial.
Photography of Clients
You must have explicit verbal or written consent from any client before posting their image — including photos of just their hands — to any public-facing social media account. Do not post content that reveals a client's identity without their permission. A simple "Is it okay if I share this on my Instagram?" before posting is required.
Cio Bela Branding on Social Media
While contracted, you may identify yourself as a nail artist "at Cio Bela" in your social media bios and content. Upon termination or resignation of your contractor agreement, all references to Cio Bela must be removed from your profile, bio, and highlights within 24 hours, as outlined in your Contractor Agreement. You may retain portfolio content of your completed work.
Content Standards
Any content that features the Cio Bela space, brand, or name — whether in a tag, caption, or visual — must reflect our brand positively. Do not post content from the salon that is politically divisive, sexually explicit, discriminatory, or that portrays Cio Bela or its staff in a negative light. Such posts are grounds for immediate review of your contractor status.
✓ Great Content
Before & after nail sets with client consent
Salon aesthetic, your station, product flat-lays
Tagging Cio Bela on work done in our space
Behind-the-scenes of your craft (process videos)
Booking links through the salon's official platform
✕ Not Permitted
Client photos without consent
Negative posts about Cio Bela, management, or other contractors
Directing followers to book outside the salon's system
Using Cio Bela's branding after contract termination
Sharing any client's personal information publicly

Section Six
Guest & Visitor Policy
Not yet reviewed
▾
Cio Bela is a professional service environment. Every person on the floor — whether a client, contractor, or visitor — contributes to the atmosphere. Our guest policy exists to protect that experience for everyone.
Personal Visitors
Contractors may receive brief personal visits from friends or family, provided the visit does not disrupt the salon environment, take up client seating, or extend beyond a casual check-in. Personal visitors may not linger at your station while you are serving a client. Lengthy personal visits during working hours are not appropriate in a professional salon setting.
Children on Premises
Children are welcome as clients or when accompanied by a parent or guardian receiving a service. Contractors may not bring children to the salon as a childcare arrangement. Children left unattended in the waiting area or roaming the salon floor is not permitted and poses a safety risk in a chemical-use environment.
Non-Contracted Individuals Performing Services
Only licensed, contracted individuals may perform services within Cio Bela. No contractor may allow an unlicensed individual to perform any service at their station — including practice, training sessions, or informal demonstrations — without prior written approval from Salon Management. This is a TDLR compliance requirement and a non-negotiable condition of your contract.
